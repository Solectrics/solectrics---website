Make the Most of your Solar

Over and above your solar system, the great news is that we can add simple timing and control devices to larger appliances and EV chargers. They help your home use more power while the sun is producing it—reducing grid use and bringing your electricity bills down even further.

You do not need to automate everything. We look at the appliances that use the most energy, then suggest the changes that are likely to make a real difference in your home.

The simple idea: generate and use more of your solar energy in daylight hours to save on your bills.

YOUR SOLAR-SMART HOME
⏱Appliance timers and smart switching

Run suitable larger appliances during your strongest solar hours instead of in the evening. Depending on the appliance and installation, this may use a timer, controller, relay or another safely installed switching device.

🚗Solar-aware EV charging

Schedule or control EV charging so more of the car's energy comes from daytime solar—or from a lower-cost electricity period when solar is not available. We also consider charging speed, available supply and the other electrical loads in your home.

▥Energy monitoring

See when your home is importing, exporting and using the most power. Clear information makes it easier to change everyday habits and decide which upgrade is worth doing next.

≈Pool, spa and pump scheduling

Pumps and heating can be significant loads. Where practical, their operating times can be shifted toward solar hours while still keeping the system working as it should.

▣Battery-ready controls

You may not need a battery on day one. We can help prepare the electrical design and controls so storage can be added later without losing sight of how the home already uses energy.

⚡Load management

When several large appliances could run together, sensible load management can help them share the available supply and prioritise what matters most.

The big opportunity

HOT WATER HEAT PUMP

  • Use around one-third of the energy of conventional electric-resistance water heating.
  • Can be about 60% cheaper to run.
  • Work especially well with rooftop solar.
  • Are generally the cheapest option over their lifetime, despite a higher upfront cost.

EECA says water heating makes up around one-third of household energy bills, so it is a very worthwhile place to look for savings.

Solectrics can source and install a hot-water heat pump that suits your household, your hot-water use and your property.

We help select the right type and size, plan how it will work with your solar, and coordinate the complete installation.

Hot water is often one of a household's largest electricity uses.

A conventional cylinder creates heat directly from electricity.

A hot-water heat pump works differently: it gathers warmth from the surrounding air and transfers that heat into the water.

Hot-water heat pumps are still relatively new technology in New Zealand. We assess the cylinder, outdoor-unit position, noise, plumbing and electrical requirements. Solectrics manages the electrical work and coordinates the required plumbing with a suitably qualified plumber.

Independent guidance: EECA — hot-water heat pumps for your home.

Solar-powered comfort

HEAT PUMP + smart controls

An efficient heat pump can warm the house while solar is available. Your home then carries some of that warmth into the evening, which may reduce the heating needed after solar production fades. EECA says a heat pump is usually the best heating option for larger rooms, recommends using a timer, and suggests a maximum winter thermostat setting of 20–21°C. Good insulation and draught control help the warmth last longer.
