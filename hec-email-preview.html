Three pathways worth discussing

1. Solar Start: Solar panels and an inverter. Your home uses solar electricity when the panels are generating, with grid electricity covering any shortfall — particularly in the evening. Battery storage may be added later if the system is designed with compatible equipment.

2. Solar Smart: Solar panels and an inverter, together with suitable timers and smart controls to help use more solar electricity as it is generated. Depending on your home and tariff, this might include scheduling hot water, EV charging and other flexible loads during solar-producing hours.

3. Solar Smart + Battery: Solar panels, a compatible inverter and battery storage to keep available surplus solar electricity for later use, including after sunset. With the right backup equipment and electrical design, the system also powers selected essential circuits during a grid outage. How long they can run depends on battery charge, usable capacity, essential loads and available solar generation — particularly in winter.

These are options to explore, not a final recommendation or formal quote.
